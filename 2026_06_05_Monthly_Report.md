## Computational Design

- **Seek-CAD: Self-Refined Generative Parametric CAD via DeepSeek Local Inference**: A May 2026 preprint introduces Seek-CAD, which leverages DeepSeek-based local inference to generate self-refined parametric CAD sequences — the discrete command-parameter language used by engineering modeling software like SolidWorks and Fusion 360. Unlike text-to-mesh approaches that produce static, non-editable geometry, Seek-CAD produces full parametric feature trees with proper constraints and references. The self-refinement loop iteratively corrects invalid sequences by checking against CAD kernel rules, a novel approach that dramatically improves geometric validity without requiring human correction. This directly addresses the longstanding gap between language-model outputs and engineering-grade CAD ready for modification and manufacture.
  - Sources: [Seek-CAD Preprint (arXiv:2505.17702)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.17702) · [Leo AI – Best AI for CAD Generation 2026](https://www.getleo.ai/blog/best-ai-for-cad-generation-2026)

Standard transformers struggle with the long discrete sequences that define complex mechanical parts; Mamba's selective state-space mechanism handles these lengths efficiently without quadratic attention cost. The encoder-decoder + GAN architecture outperforms prior transformer baselines on complex geometries and is the first SSM-based approach to earn conference recognition in the CAD generation space. Code and data availability will accelerate follow-on research.

## 3D Printing / Additive Manufacturing

- **Harvard Rotational Multi-Material Printing: Programmable Muscle-Like Soft Actuators**: Harvard researchers developed a novel rotational multi-material 3D printing method that creates muscle-like soft robotic structures whose motion direction — twist, lift, bend — is encoded during fabrication. The rotational approach deposits alternating stiff and flexible materials in helical patterns, embedding directional actuation behavior into the material architecture analogously to how fiber orientation in biological muscle determines movement direction. This bypasses the constraint of conventional layer-by-layer deposition, which limits the axes along which programmed compliance can act. The result is soft actuators with significantly more complex and controllable motion profiles than previously achievable with extrusion-based methods, and a direct fabrication route for bio-inspired artificial muscles.
  - Sources: [Tom's Hardware – Harvard Rotational Multi-Material Printing for Soft Robotics](https://www.tomshardware.com/3d-printing/harvard-researchers-develop-novel-3d-printing-method-for-soft-robotics-rotational-multi-material-method-creates-muscle-like-structures-that-can-be-programmed-to-twist-lift-or-bend)

- **Multimaterial 3D Printing of Soft and Stretchable Electronics with Embedded Conductors (Advanced Science 2026)**: A 2026 Advanced Science paper presents a two-photon polymerization (2PP)-compatible resin integrating the conducting polymer PEDOT:PSS and multi-walled carbon nanotubes within a PEGDA hydrogel matrix, enabling single-run printing of soft, stretchable, and electrically conductive microstructures. The approach overcomes the three main limitations of prior multimaterial electronic printing: low electrical conductivity, filler aggregation, and loss of optical transparency. Combining fine 2PP resolution with multi-material capability in a single print run enables microscale electronic structures embedded in flexible, biocompatible substrates. Key target applications include neural biointerfaces, wearable biosensors, and microscale energy harvesting devices.
  - Sources: [Advanced Science – Multimaterial 3D Printing of Soft/Stretchable Electronics](https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/advs.202513208?af=R) · [npj Advanced Manufacturing – Printing Orientation and Interfacial Design](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44334-026-00075-y)

- **Sequential Multi-Printer Fabrication: Breaking Single-Process Material Compatibility Limits**: A method in Advanced Materials Technologies combines masked stereolithography (LCD) with Xolography sequentially across two printers, enabling integration of chemically and optically incompatible materials — such as shape-memory polymer composites and stimuli-responsive hydrogels — in a single physical construct. These material combinations are impossible to co-print on any single system due to incompatible cure conditions, but sequential handoff between dedicated printers relaxes the compatibility constraint. The result is prints with multi-stimulus responsive behaviors (e.g., thermally triggered shape change combined with hydrogel swelling) that encode complex programmable physical behaviors unavailable in monolithic prints. This strategy generalizes to any pair of single-material printers, making it highly accessible to labs with existing equipment.
  - Sources: [Advanced Materials Technologies – Sequential Multi-Printer Fabrication](https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/admt.202502447?af=R) · [Sovol – Multi-Material 3D Printing 2026](https://www.sovol3d.com/blogs/news/multi-material-3d-printing-in-2026)

- **Microfluidic- and Field-Assisted 3D Printing: Leveraging Physics to Expand AM Capability**: A 2026 PMC review surveys microfluidic- and field-assisted (electric, magnetic, acoustic) 3D printing, which use active physical forces during deposition to achieve spatial gradients, aligned fiber architectures, and real-time composition switching that passive extrusion cannot produce. These techniques allow printing of anisotropic composites with magnetically aligned reinforcing fibers, stiffness gradients within a single structure, and spatially programmed electrical properties. The review frames this as a frontier for combining fluidic control with multi-physics fields to fundamentally expand what AM can produce — particularly relevant for bioprinting (tissue constructs with spatially varying mechanics) and functional robotics (anisotropic soft actuators).
  - Sources: [PMC – Microfluidic- and Field-Assisted 3D Printing](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12711614/)

- **WAAM Enters Mainstream for Large-Format Metal Components**: Wire Arc Additive Manufacturing (WAAM) — which deposits metal from welding-grade wire using an electric arc — is gaining mainstream industrial adoption in 2026 for large-scale aerospace, defense, and construction parts, where casting and forging create long lead times and high material waste. WAAM3D's MiniWAAM system extends the technology to R&D labs. ## Computational Sciences

- **SOPTX: Modular, Multi-Backend Topology Optimization Framework (Communications in Computational Physics 2026)**: SOPTX, released in 2026 and published in Communications in Computational Physics, provides a unified open-source framework for topology optimization supporting multiple computational backends (CPU, GPU, varied FEM solvers) through a common API. Previously, TO practitioners had to choose between specialized single-purpose codes with incompatible interfaces, making comparison and extension difficult. SOPTX allows switching solvers and hardware without rewriting optimization logic, and its modular architecture makes it straightforward to plug in new material models, physics modules, and optimization algorithms. For researchers applying TO to new domains — soft robotics, multi-physics structures, manufacturing constraints — SOPTX significantly lowers the implementation barrier.
  - Sources: [SOPTX – Communications in Computational Physics](https://www.global-sci.com/cicp/article/view/24166)

- **SimTO: Physics-Simulation-Informed Topology Optimization for Soft Robotic Grippers**: SimTO is a 2026 framework that performs high-resolution topology optimization for compliant grippers by automatically extracting load cases from contact-based physics simulators, eliminating the manual load-specification step that is the primary bottleneck when designing grippers for unstructured grasping. The system simulates the robot's interaction with diverse object geometries to build a representative load distribution, then runs standard TO on that distribution. Conventional TO requires prescribing forces and boundary conditions upfront, which is difficult when contact mechanics are complex and object-dependent. SimTO-designed grippers demonstrated significantly better generalized grasping performance than manually-specified baselines in experiments.
  - Sources: [SimTO – arXiv:2601.19098](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19098)

- **End-to-End Quantum Algorithm for Topology Optimization**: Researchers published the first end-to-end, fault-tolerant quantum algorithm for topology optimization in structural mechanics, operating on an exponentially large Hilbert space that represents the full design variable space. The algorithm leverages quantum superposition to explore design configurations simultaneously that would require exponential classical compute to enumerate. While current hardware cannot yet execute the full algorithm at practical problem sizes, the theoretical framework is complete and demonstrates potential quantum advantage for high-resolution TO problems where the design space is extremely large. This is the first rigorous proof-of-concept establishing quantum computing as a viable path for structural optimization.
  - Sources: [IOP Science – Quantum Algorithm for Topology Optimization](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/2058-9565/ae4cfe)

- **DRL + Genetic Algorithms + FEM: Practical Hybrid Topology Optimization**: A 2026 paper presents a comprehensive TO framework combining deep reinforcement learning (DRL), genetic algorithms (GA), and finite element methods (FEM), with a key innovation: training data is generated within physics simulators on-the-fly during learning rather than requiring expensive pre-collected datasets. The hybrid approach combines DRL's heuristic learning with GA's global search and FEM's physical accuracy. Results on standard 2D and 3D structural benchmarks show competitive performance with classical solvers at reduced computational cost for repeated design tasks. The on-the-fly data generation is the practical core innovation, enabling adaptation to novel problem geometries without separate data collection phases.
  - Sources: [Science Publishing Group – DRL+GA Topology Optimization](https://www.sciencepublishinggroup.com/article/10.11648/j.ajasr.20261201.11)

- **Decomposed Fourier Neural Operator (D-FNO): 2-3× Speedup for 3D PDE Simulations**: The Decomposed Fourier Neural Operator (D-FNO), published in CMAME 2025 and now in active use, leverages tensor decomposition to reduce the computational complexity of 3D neural operator training, achieving 2-3× speedup over the Factorized FNO while maintaining accuracy. Neural operators learn resolution-invariant mappings between function spaces (e.g., boundary conditions to PDE solutions), enabling inference at speeds orders of magnitude faster than classical solvers. The D-FNO speedup is particularly significant for 3D engineering simulations — fluid dynamics, thermal analysis, structural FEA — where computational cost scales with spatial dimensions. This advance brings neural operators closer to real-time feasibility for interactive design-space exploration.
  - Sources: [arXiv – Learning Physical Operators using Neural Operators](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.23113) · [Medium – Fourier Neural Operators and PINOs](https://medium.com/@shanmuka.sadhu/paper-insights-fourier-neural-operators-and-physics-informed-neural-operators-735e05dc5dc9)

- **HAMSTER: Physics-Informed ML Predicts Hamiltonians for 50,000-Atom Systems**: A 2026 physics-informed ML framework called HAMSTER predicts quantum-mechanical Hamiltonians for complex chemical systems containing up to 50,000 atoms — several orders of magnitude larger than systems tractable with direct DFT methods. Trained on smaller systems and constrained by physical symmetry and locality principles, HAMSTER generalizes to far larger compositions including halide perovskites across temperature ranges relevant to photovoltaic applications. The ability to predict electronic structure for industrially relevant material sizes could dramatically accelerate materials discovery for energy and electronics applications. This is among the most computationally ambitious physics-informed ML results demonstrated to date.
  - Sources: [Medium – Fourier Neural Operators and Physics-Informed Neural Operators](https://medium.com/@shanmuka.sadhu/paper-insights-fourier-neural-operators-and-physics-informed-neural-operators-735e05dc5dc9) · [Nature – Physics-Informed Machine Learning Collection](https://www.nature.com/collections/jaihfcabgi)

- **PICore: Data-Efficient Neural Operator Training via Physics-Informed Coreset Selection**: PICore, published in 2026, addresses the data efficiency bottleneck in neural operator training by selecting the most informative training samples using a physics-informed residual criterion — prioritizing samples where the model has the highest physical error — without requiring full simulation runs for all candidates. Training neural operators currently demands large, expensive datasets of PDE simulations; PICore identifies a small "coreset" that captures the most physically diverse training signal. Early results demonstrate accuracy comparable to full-dataset training with substantially fewer samples, which is critical for domains like aerodynamics and heat transfer where each simulation run is computationally costly and slow.
  - Sources: [arXiv – PICore Physics-Informed Coreset Selection](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.17151)

## Soft Robotics & Compliant Mechanisms

- **Origami-Inspired Electrohydraulic Soft Actuators: Jumping 13.9 Body Heights (JAP January 2026)**: A January 2026 Journal of Applied Physics paper introduces origami-inspired electrohydraulic soft actuators achieving multimodal locomotion — including jumps reaching 13.9 body heights, crawling at 1.23 body lengths per second, and compliant grasping — from a single actuator design. The mechanism displaces incompressible dielectric fluid through electrostatic zipping, forcing it into origami-patterned chambers where pressure-driven unfolding generates output torque: a novel combination of electrostatic actuation, fluidic force transmission, and origami geometry. The 13.9 body-height jump is exceptional for a soft robot, approaching the performance of rigid jumping mechanisms, while the multimodal capability (jump, crawl, grasp) from a single actuator type avoids the complexity of switching mechanisms.
  - Sources: [JAP – Origami Electrohydraulic Soft Actuators](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/jap/article/139/2/025109/3377304/Origami-inspired-electrohydraulic-soft-actuators)

- **Unified Framework for Soft Inflatable Fabric Actuators**: A 2026 PMC paper presents a unified design framework systematically relating geometric configuration (seam patterns, material layups) to deformation mode — stiffening, contraction, bending, twisting, elongation, and omnidirectional motion — within a single fabric-based inflatable actuator fabrication approach. Previously, each motion type required a distinct actuator design and process; this framework allows selection or combination of deformation modes by varying seam patterns alone, using the same fabrication workflow throughout. Lightweight, compliant, and manufacturable from commodity textiles, fabric-based inflatable actuators are particularly attractive for wearable assistive devices, soft exosuits, and human-safe physical interaction where low mass and inherent compliance during contact are essential.
  - Sources: [PMC – Unified Framework for Soft Inflatable Fabric Actuators](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12644749/)

- **Impulsive Actuation for Soft Robots: Review of Fast Dynamics Strategies (npj Robotics 2025)**: An npj Robotics review systematically examines impulsive actuation in soft robots — the generation of fast, explosive motions — categorizing direct fast-responsive actuators and power amplification mechanisms including snap-through bistability and stored elastic energy release. The review identifies HASEL (Hydraulically Amplified Self-Healing Electrostatic) actuators as a key enabling technology, where electric fields displace enclosed dielectric liquid for actuation, bypassing the speed penalty of external pumping. Demonstrated applications include ballistic object launching and catching, jumping over high obstacles, and pumping for artificial hearts. The synthesis provides a design roadmap for soft robots needing dynamics competitive with rigid-body systems in speed, not just compliance.
  - Sources: [npj Robotics – Impulsive Actuation for Soft Robots](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44182-025-00045-0)

- **Advances in Dielectric Elastomer and Fluid Actuators: Comprehensive npj Robotics Review (2026)**: A 2026 npj Robotics review covers material innovations, fabrication methods, actuation modes, failure mechanisms, and applications of both dielectric elastomer actuators (DEAs) and dielectric fluid actuators (DFAs/HASELs), including deployment in artificial muscles, biomimetic robots, haptic interfaces, adaptive optics, and biomedical devices. A key performance finding: circular HASEL designs achieve 4× the areal strain of a comparable-shape DEA at the same applied voltage (11 kV), a substantial advantage for large-deformation actuation. A patent landscape analysis shows dielectric-based actuators have the lowest IP density of the three major soft actuator categories (pneumatic, SMA, DEA), representing the greatest opportunity for foundational IP development as the field matures.
  - Sources: [npj Robotics – Advancing Soft Robotics: DEA and DFA Review 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44182-026-00074-3)

- **Auxetic Meta-Structure Soft Locomotive Robot: Mechanical Intelligence via Material Geometry**: Published in npj Robotics, this work demonstrates a soft locomotive robot whose body is structured as an auxetic metamaterial — with negative Poisson's ratio — that expands laterally when stretched axially. The auxetic geometry creates coupled deformations translating simple periodic pneumatic inflation into stable, directed locomotion without requiring multiple independent actuators or trajectory control. The robot traverses flat and uneven surfaces driven by a single periodic pressure input, encoding locomotion behavior into material geometry rather than computational control. This is a compelling example of mechanical intelligence: offloading control complexity into structure design, with direct implications for deployments where onboard compute and power are severely constrained.
  - Sources: [npj Robotics – Auxetic Meta-Structure Soft Locomotive Robot](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44182-025-00050-3)

- **4D-Printed Shape Memory Polymer Composites: Multi-Stimulus Smart Structures (ACS Applied Polymer Materials 2026)**: A 2026 ACS Applied Polymer Materials review covers 4D-printed shape memory polymer composites (SMPCs), where functional fillers (conductive particles, magnetic nanoparticles, light-absorbing dyes) broaden stimulus modalities — heat, electric current, light, magnetism, chemical environment — for triggering programmed shape recovery. The paper covers all major 4D printing processes (FDM, SLS, DIW, vat photopolymerization) and identifies process-structure-property relationships for each. Key emerging applications include deployable aerospace structures (solar arrays, antenna panels), shape-changing biomedical scaffolds (vascular stents), and self-actuating grippers responding to environmental cues. The convergence with multi-material printing is identified as the most transformative near-term frontier.
  - Sources: [ACS Applied Polymer Materials – 4D-Printed Shape Memory Polymer Composites](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acsapm.5c02816) · [PMC – Advanced Design of SMPs in Biomedical/Soft Robotics](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12845505/)

- **Bioinspired Underwater Soft Robots: Biology-to-Robotics and Back (npj Robotics Review 2026)**: A 2026 npj Robotics review takes stock of underwater soft robots inspired by marine biology — fish, rays, octopus, jellyfish — analyzing how effectively biological principles (undulatory propulsion, jet propulsion, fin-based steering) have been translated into engineered systems. It finds that structural inspiration (body shape, appendage geometry) is well-established but translation of biological sensing strategies and neural computation lags significantly. The review proposes a "biology to robotics and back" research loop where robotic models test biological hypotheses and vice versa, accelerating progress on both sides. Practically, it catalogs best-performing locomotion mechanisms per environmental condition, offering a useful design reference for underwater robotics.
  - Sources: [npj Robotics – Bioinspired Underwater Soft Robots 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44182-026-00088-x)
