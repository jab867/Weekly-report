## 3D Printing / Additive Manufacturing

- **Harvard's Rotational Multi-Material Printhead Embeds Muscle-Like Fiber Patterns**: A team led by Natalie Larson and Jackson Wilt at Harvard SEAS built a printhead with four ink cartridges feeding a single continuously rotating nozzle, which embeds helical, muscle-like fiber patterns directly into extruded filament as it prints. By tuning rotation speed, flow rate, and nozzle geometry, the team programmed exactly how a tough polyurethane shell and a soft poloxamer core combine internally, producing pneumatic soft-robot parts — including a flower-like actuator that unfurls and a curling-finger gripper — that twist, bend, or curl in a predetermined direction when inflated, without multi-step assembly. Published in *Advanced Materials*.
  - Sources: [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/3d-printing/harvard-researchers-develop-novel-3d-printing-method-for-soft-robotics-rotational-multi-material-method-creates-muscle-like-structures-that-can-be-programmed-to-twist-lift-or-bend) · [Wiley/Advanced Materials](https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/adma.202510141) · [3Dnatives](https://www.3dnatives.com/en/3d-printed-soft-robots-harvard-69366-2/)

- **EPFL's HT-VAM Holographic Printer Achieves 70x Efficiency Gain in Volumetric Bioprinting**: EPFL's Laboratory of Applied Photonic Devices built a new volumetric printing platform using a MEMS-based phase-only light modulator to directly sculpt laser phase for the first time in volumetric printing, raising optical efficiency roughly 70-fold to about 24%. This lets low-power lasers solidify millimeter-scale objects in seconds and centimeter-scale cell-laden structures in minutes; the team bioprinted a life-sized human ear and confirmed embedded human cells stayed viable after six days, demonstrating near-clinical-scale tissue fabrication. EPFL is commercializing the tech via spin-off Readily3D and bio-ink maker BIO INX.
  - Sources: [VoxelMatters](https://www.voxelmatters.com/epfl-70x-efficiency-gain-holographic-volumetric-3d-printing/) · [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1128753) · [manufactur3dmag](https://manufactur3dmag.com/epfl-holographic-volumetric-3d-printing/)

- **University of Twente Develops Wave-Inspired Toolpaths for Support-Free Overhangs**: Researchers modeled extrusion paths after wave-propagation/diffraction physics rather than conventional concentric or arc-based infill, producing continuous paths that naturally wrap around corners and holes on standard 3-axis FDM printers. This solves a long-standing problem — printing horizontal cantilevered overhangs without sacrificial support material — and validation showed wave paths produce significantly less sagging and fewer gaps than arc-based toolpaths as overhang difficulty increases; the method is already implemented in open-source forks of PrusaSlicer and OrcaSlicer.
  - Sources: [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S277236902600040X) · [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6640458) · [GitHub Implementation](https://github.com/stmcculloch/PrusaSlicer-WaveOverhangs)

- **Mastrex Launches Sub-$200K Industrial Metal LPBF Printer**: New Jersey-based Mastrex launched the MX300, an industrial metal laser powder bed fusion printer priced at $185,000, notably lower than typical industrial metal AM systems. It offers a 300×300×350mm build volume, dual 500W lasers, and supports aluminum, Inconel, and stainless steel for aerospace, defense, and medical applications, part of a broader 2026 trend toward more price-accessible metal AM hardware.
  - Sources: [3DPrint.com](https://3dprint.com/326493/3d-printing-news-briefs-6-6-2026/)

- **Print Orientation Research Targets Rigid-Flexible Interface Bonding in Multi-Material Parts**: A study in *npj Advanced Manufacturing* addresses a persistent multi-material printing failure point — weak adhesion at interfaces between mechanically dissimilar materials (e.g., rigid plus flexible) that causes premature debonding under load. The researchers show that controlling print orientation and interface geometry can meaningfully improve bond strength between stiff and soft printed regions, directly relevant to soft robotics, wearables, and adaptive structures.

## Computational Sciences

- **"Mollifier Layers" Tackle Inverse PDE Problems With Less Memory and More Stability**: Penn Engineering researchers (Vinayak Vinayak, Ananyae K. Bhartari, Vivek B. Shenoy) developed an architecture-agnostic neural network module that replaces standard automatic differentiation with convolutional operations built on analytically-defined mollifiers (smoothing functions dating to Kurt Otto Friedrichs in the 1940s) to compute high-order derivatives needed for inverse PDE problems. Attaching only at the output layer with no architectural modification, it dramatically reduces memory use and improves stability under noisy data — a long-standing bottleneck — with potential applications in genetics and weather forecasting. Published in Transactions on Machine Learning Research, slated for NeurIPS 2026.
  - Sources: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11682) · [Penn Engineering](https://www.seas.upenn.edu/stories/ai-method-tackles-one-of-sciences-hardest-math-problems/) · [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-05-ai-tackles-math-brutal-problems.html)

- **Aalto University Simulates a 268-Million-Site Quasicrystal With a Quantum-Inspired Tensor-Network Algorithm**: Aalto researchers developed a tensor-network algorithm that simulated a topological quasicrystal with over 268 million lattice sites — a problem otherwise requiring more than a quadrillion numbers, beyond the reach of the world's most powerful classical supercomputers. The method exploits an exponential speed-up analogous to quantum computation while running on classical hardware, enabling construction of "super-moiré" quasicrystals orders of magnitude larger than prior techniques allowed, with stated applications in dissipationless electronics and topological-qubit materials.
  - Sources: [Aalto University](https://www.aalto.fi/en/news/ability-to-harness-quantum-speed-gains-now-within-sight-after-researchers-solve-massive-simulation) · [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/04/17/aalto-team-reports-new-algorithm-tackles-quasicrystal-calculations-beyond-classical-limits/) · [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-04-quantum-algorithm-million-site-quasicrystal.html)

- **ANN Framework Generates Fail-Safe Topology-Optimized Structures**: Published in *Structural and Multidisciplinary Optimization*, this framework uses artificial neural networks to convert a standard minimum-compliance topology-optimized design into a fail-safe design that accounts for the combinatorial explosion of failure scenarios (local damage or member loss) that fail-safe analysis normally requires. A post-optimization connectivity step preserves damage tolerance, reportedly achieving median compliance reductions of roughly 96% versus non-fail-safe designs; the authors released their training dataset publicly for reproducibility.
  - Sources: [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00158-026-04362-2)

- **GPU-Accelerated Immersed Boundary Solver Hits 20x Speedup on Billion-Point Grids**: Researchers (Kumar, Romero, Seo, Fatica, Mittal) released a GPU-optimized implementation of the sharp-interface immersed boundary method (built on the ViCar3D framework, using OpenACC/CUDA/NCCL/MPI) for simulating flow around complex stationary and moving bodies on Cartesian grids. Tested from ~10 million to ~1 billion grid points, it achieves a 20x speedup over the existing CPU implementation with over 90% multi-GPU scaling efficiency, demonstrated on turbulent fluid-structure interaction for a flapping bat wing in flight.
  - Sources: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04335) · [arXiv (companion solver)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17287)

- **Quanscient and Haiqu Cut Qubit Overhead for Quantum CFD**: Quanscient and Haiqu announced a new algorithm, run on IBM quantum hardware, that reduces the qubit count needed for complex computational fluid dynamics simulations — a step toward making quantum-computed fluid simulation practical at larger problem scales by targeting one of quantum CFD's core barriers: qubit overhead growing faster than simulation fidelity.
  - Sources: [AZoQuantum](https://www.azoquantum.com/News.aspx?newsID=11097)

## Soft Robotics & Compliant Mechanisms

- **Inclined-Cut Kirigami Converts Stretching Into Spontaneous Twist**: A University of Osaka team created cylindrical kirigami sheets with periodic, parallel *inclined* laser cuts (rather than the conventional perpendicular/parallel cut patterns), so that stretching the cylinders causes them to spontaneously twist and rotate — a "chirality-induced tension–rotation coupling" that converts a simple pulling force into rotational motion, functioning as a passive, motorless twist actuator. Some configurations also show auxetic behavior (lateral expansion under elongation), and the work breaks from decades of kirigami designs limited to orthogonal cuts, opening a simple, scalable geometric route to soft actuators for robotics and adaptive medical devices.
  - Sources: [Royal Society Open Science](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.250983) · [TechXplore](https://techxplore.com/news/2026-06-inclined-kirigami-path-soft-robots.html)

- **BOSPA: Bistable Origami Pneumatic Actuator Drives Four Distinct Soft-Legged Robots**: Researchers built a 3D-printed, inflatable Miura-origami tube actuator with two parallel air chambers, rubber elastic rings, and rigid 3D-printed connectors to create snap-through bistability. Programming differential air pressure across the chambers lets a single actuator unit produce linear extension, bending, or coupled bending/extension with snap-through transitions; the team built four robots from it — a jumping robot reaching 0.65 body-lengths per hop at 1.25 Hz, a crawler achieving 1.22 body-lengths/sec and climbing 14° inclines, and a tripod robot capable of rolling, peristaltic crawling, or leg-propelled locomotion.
  - Sources: [SAGE Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/21695172261424024)

- **Bidirectional Origami-Pneumatic Actuator Breaks the One-Direction-Bending Limit**: Published in *Actuators* (MDPI), this work tackles a common limitation of origami-pneumatic actuators — typically only bending in one direction — by designing a folded-tube actuator architecture engineered for reversible, bidirectional bending within a compact, predictable structural form, broadening the actuation envelope without sacrificing fold-pattern programmability.
  - Sources: [MDPI Actuators](https://doi.org/10.3390/act15060320)

- **KAIST Hybrid Shape-Memory Actuator Achieves Sub-Second, Motorless Reversible Recovery**: A KAIST team led by Prof. Seong Su Kim combined a carbon-fiber-reinforced, chemically tuned two-way shape memory polymer with optimized thermomechanical design to achieve sub-second shape change and full reversible recovery without a motor — notably fast for a thermally-driven shape memory device. The actuator is being positioned for space-grade applications such as deployable structures and grippers, where motorless, lightweight, reversible actuation is highly valued.
  - Sources: [Wiley: Advanced Functional Materials](https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/adfm.75166) · [TechXplore](https://techxplore.com/news/2026-03-robotic-motors-shifting-actuator.html)

- **New Origami Pattern Resolves the Flexibility-vs-Stiffness Trade-off in Foldable Structures**: New research addresses a long-standing origami design tension: smooth, curved foldable structures tend to be soft and floppy, while strong, stiff ones tend to be faceted and hard to re-tune once built. The new pattern is presented as resolving this trade-off, enabling flat sheets to fold into structurally strong, load-bearing 3D geometries while retaining tunable, smoother curvature — relevant to deployable structures such as shelters, space hardware, and architecture that need both foldability and post-deployment rigidity.
  - Sources: [TechXplore](https://techxplore.com/news/2026-05-origami-pattern-flat-sheets-3d.html)

## Biology & Medical Research

- **Intellia's In Vivo CRISPR Therapy Posts a Potential Functional Cure for Hereditary Angioedema**: A single IV infusion of lonvoguran ziclumeran (lonvo-z), an in vivo CRISPR-Cas9 therapy delivered via lipid nanoparticles, permanently knocks out the KLKB1 gene in liver hepatocytes, halting bradykinin-driven swelling attacks at the genetic source. In the global Phase 3 HAELO trial (80 patients), a single 50mg dose cut attack rates by 87% (mean monthly rate 0.26 vs. 2.10) and rendered 62% of patients completely attack-free over 6 months with no serious safety signals — a likely "global first" one-time genetic cure rather than chronic management, with a rolling FDA filing underway and a potential US launch in H1 2027.
  - Sources: [BioPharma Dive](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/intellia-gene-editing-hereditary-angioedema-lonvo-z-results/818503/) · [STAT News](https://www.statnews.com/2026/04/27/intellia-therapeutics-phase-3-trial-hereditary-angioedema-crispr-treatment/) · [NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2600931)

- **Daraxonrasib Nearly Doubles Survival in Metastatic Pancreatic Cancer**: Daraxonrasib is a pan-RAS(ON) inhibitor that, unlike earlier mutation-specific KRAS inhibitors (e.g., sotorasib for G12C only), blocks active-state RAS signaling regardless of the specific mutation, with activity even in RAS-wild-type tumors. In the Phase 3 RASolute 302 trial (~500 patients), presented at the ASCO plenary session and covered widely through June, median overall survival nearly doubled to 13.2 months versus 6.7 months with chemotherapy (hazard ratio 0.40), with objective response rate nearly tripling — a transformative result in a cancer where second-line treatment has barely moved survival in decades.
  - Sources: [Revolution Medicines](https://ir.revmed.com/news-releases/news-release-details/daraxonrasib-demonstrates-unprecedented-overall-survival-benefit) · [NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2605555) · [AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/rasolute-302-brings-a-transformative-moment-on-pancreatic-cancer-a-60-improvement-in-overall-survival)

- **Just 10 Viral Particles Can Infect Dairy Cattle With H5N1**: A study released mid-June found that an extremely low infectious dose — roughly 10 viral particles — of the H5N1 strain responsible for hundreds of US dairy cattle outbreaks is sufficient to cause infection in cows. This helps explain the explosive, hard-to-contain spread of H5N1 through dairy herds and has implications for biosecurity protocols and pandemic-preparedness risk modeling, given H5N1's documented capacity for occasional human spillover.
  - Sources: [Works in Progress](https://www.worksinprogress.news/p/whats-new-in-biology-june-2026)

- **Microbial Cell-Free DNA Sequencing Predicts Bloodstream Infections in Leukemia Patients Days Before Symptoms**: St. Jude researchers used plasma microbial cell-free DNA sequencing on daily blood samples from 158 high-risk pediatric leukemia patients, identifying the causative pathogen up to 3 days before clinical onset in 51.9% of bloodstream infection episodes. Bloodstream infections are a leading cause of treatment delays and mortality in immunocompromised children undergoing chemotherapy, and early pathogen identification could enable pre-emptive, targeted antimicrobial therapy instead of reactive broad-spectrum treatment.
  - Sources: [St. Jude](https://www.stjude.org/media-resources/news-releases/2026-medicine-science-news/cell-free-dna-offers-early-warning-for-bloodstream-infections-in-kids-with-leukemia.html) · [Hematology Advisor](https://www.hematologyadvisor.com/news/plasma-microbial-dna-acute-leukemia-sequencing-predict-infection/)

- **Single-Cell Analysis Reveals Shared Cellular Origin Across Three Rare Childhood Cancers**: St. Jude researchers performed single-cell resolution analysis across pineoblastoma, medulloblastoma, and retinoblastoma and found that all three pediatric cancers share an abnormally expressed photoreceptor-like gene signature and a common dependence on this gene set for survival — tumor cells died when these genes were knocked out. This is surprising because these cancers arise in anatomically distinct tissues (pineal gland, cerebellum, retina) yet appear to share a developmental cell-of-origin, opening a possible cross-tumor therapeutic target for multiple hard-to-treat pediatric cancers simultaneously.
  - Sources: [St. Jude](https://www.stjude.org/media-resources/news-releases/2026-medicine-science-news/shared-origins-illuminate-potential-dependency-across-brain-tumor-types.html)

## Key Takeaways

- **Agentic AI is moving from chat to direct manipulation across both software and physical systems**: Anthropic's Opus 4.8 "dynamic workflows," Autodesk's Fusion MCP integration, the REER plugin's direct scene-graph editing in Rhino, and FANUC's natural-language-to-robot-code demo all point to the same shift — AI agents acting directly on CAD geometry, robot motion, and codebases rather than merely suggesting next steps.
